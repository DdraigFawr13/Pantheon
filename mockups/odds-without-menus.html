Odds without option menus

Three ways to show a check's odds before you act, without a list of tapped options. Each one uses your game: Ddraig at the bottom of the Sunken Stair, the end of turn 5, with the humming sack in front of him. Every percentage on this page came from the engine, using your save.

A · Preview, then go

You type anything. If it needs a roll, the game shows the odds first and waits. "Go" rolls exactly that. Anything else backs off, and nothing is rolled. Talking, asking and looking never pause.

Ddraig looked at the sack, at the dead man, at the rope going up into the dark, and started counting what they could carry in one trip.

I work the sack loose from under the beam, slowly, without opening it.
BEFORE YOU ACT
Agility check, hard: 38%
At stake: the sack. Nothing has been rolled yet.
Or type something else. Rewording the same action keeps these odds; a different approach gets new ones.
Go
Agility check: 38% chance, rolled 22: success

He slid two fingers under the knot and eased the canvas out from under the beam a finger's width at a time…

Buys

  • Full freedom: any action, in your own words.
  • You see the real odds of exactly what you typed, before anything is rolled.

Costs

  • One extra round on risky actions only (estimate: 20 to 40 s each, not measured).
  • The narrator picks the stat and difficulty from your words, so a vague line can get a stat you didn't expect. You see it before it rolls, and can back off.

B · Odds in the closing thought

The scene already ends with Ddraig weighing what to do next. The engine stamps the odds onto the approaches he considers, the way it prints the dice today. You type whatever you like.

Ddraig looked at the sack, at the dead man, at the rope going up into the dark, and started counting.

He could work the sack loose from under the beam (Agility 38%), and maybe learn why it hummed. He could haul the first body up the rope while his arms still worked (Vigor 39%). Or he could ask Senna what else Tam was saying (no roll).

None of those include dying down here. Good. Keep it that way.

Buys

  • No extra round: the odds are there when you read the scene.
  • It reads like the story, not like a menu.

Costs

  • An action Ddraig didn't consider gets no odds before it rolls.
  • It is still a short list of choices, just written as prose. It may pull you toward the listed ones, the habit you wanted to break.

C · Both

The closing thought carries odds, as in B. If you type something it didn't cover, you get the preview from A first.

…Or he could ask Senna what else Tam was saying (no roll).

Before anything, I shore up the beam over the pocket with timber from the slope.
BEFORE YOU ACT
Intellect check, routine: 58%
At stake: a collapse. Nothing has been rolled yet.
An approach from the closing thought rolls at once, at the odds already shown.

Buys

  • The odds are visible up front, and nothing ever rolls unseen.

Costs

  • Two mechanisms to build, test and keep in step.
  • It shares B's list effect.
My pick: A. It is the only one that never puts a list in front of you, so it best fits wanting to play less on rails. B is the fastest. C is the most complete, and the most to build.
