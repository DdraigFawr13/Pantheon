## Turns

### Turn 1

**Player:** [The game begins. Open with The Dual Offer. Narrate the collapse of the Stair as it happens and the fall into the dark. Then each of the two gods speaks to the character directly, with a big personality in its own distinct voice, hinting at its Oath's gift and Burden without spelling them out; the game's menu states the terms. End with both voices waiting for an answer.]

Ddraig Fawr had spent the morning on an upturned crate at the lip of the Sunken Stair, tallying what the Rifters carried up. Three crates of rift-glass, one bundle of beast hide, a sack the team leader would not open in front of a clerk. Ddraig wrote it all down anyway. That was the job.

The first crack sounded like someone stepping on a dry stick. The second one went through the whole mill floor.

The Stair dropped. Ddraig went with it, ledger and crate and all, past the Rifters shouting below, past the old millstone turning end over end, and into a dark that did not stop when it should have.

When it did stop, there was stone on every side and very little air. Ddraig took stock the way a clerk would. Vigor 30 was the best thing on the sheet, and it was not going to move a mill. Intellect 29 was good for counting how long the air would last. The answer was not long.

*So this is how the tally closes. Short.*

Then something answered a wish Ddraig had not said out loud.

The first voice came up through the stone, slow and soft, like soil settling over a seed. "Easy, little counter. Everyone comes to me in the end. I am in no hurry for you." It sounded almost kind. "Above you, the Rifters are dying badly. Somebody should hear their last words. Somebody should make sure they are laid down properly, and stay down."

The second voice arrived like water filling the shaft, warm and enormous and not especially interested in Ddraig in particular. "Ignore the gravedigger. Stillness is how you ended up under a mill, darling. Come with me and you will never be stuck anywhere again. The weather will tell you its secrets. You simply will not be allowed to stay."

The two voices did not like sharing the dark. Ddraig could feel it, a pressure from two sides.

There was time, just, to ask them something. Ask what each one actually wanted in return. Ask why a clerk was worth the trouble. Or try a bargain, which the clerk's apprentice in Ddraig suspected neither god would take kindly.

Both voices waited, and the air got thinner.

### Turn 2

**Player:** [I swear the Turning Tide Oath to Ysmere. The Ledger opens. Narrate the aftermath.]

"The Tide," Ddraig said. "I'll take the Tide."

"Obviously," Ysmere said, delighted, the way a very large person is delighted to win at cards. "The gravedigger will sulk for a century. Let him. Hold still, darling, this part tickles."

It did not tickle. It felt like being picked up and put down a little to the left of where he had been. Then a page opened behind his eyes, written in his own handwriting.

**Ddraig Fawr. Human. Tier 1, Level 1. Elementalist. Enchanter.**
**HP 70/70. Mana 63/63. Stamina 59/59.**
**Affinities: Storm 1, Tide 1. Oath: Turning Tide.**

Under the class there was one more line. *Attuned:* and then nothing, like a form somebody had forgotten to finish.

Ddraig read it twice, because he was a clerk and you always check the sum. Elementalist meant throwing the weather at people. He had two weathers to choose from. Storm, which he had been born with and which had never done anything but make his hair stand up before thunder. Or Tide, which a goddess had handed him thirty seconds ago and which came with strings.

*Pick the one you know, or the one your new boss likes. Classic clerk problem.*

"You'll know the rain before it knows itself," Ysmere went on. "Tomorrow, by the way. And you'll keep moving. No roots, darling. Roots are how people end up under mills."

The stone above him shifted. Grey light came through, then a hand, then a broad face with a Registrar's chain around the neck.

"Got a live one!" the face shouted. "Clerk! Can you climb?"

He could. Vigor 30 was finally useful. He came up into the ruins of the mill with half of Cinder Ford standing around the hole. Registrar Hale Dunmore, who signed his wages, looked at him with the narrow expression of a man several tiers up who could tell when somebody's tier had just changed.

Across the rubble a Rifter he did not know sat on a broken beam, grey with dust, watching him. She did not look surprised.

Dunmore lowered his voice. "You came out of that with a Ledger. Register before someone else decides what you are. And the crew's sack went down with you. The Tallyhouse will want it."

Registering meant fees and questions. The sack meant going back toward the hole.

### Turn 3

**Player:** Reach for the storm and see what answers

Ddraig decided that if he was going to be an Elementalist, he should find out what that meant before someone asked him to prove it.

He did what he did with any unfamiliar ledger. He started with what he already had. Storm 1 had been with him since he was born. It was the itch behind his teeth before thunder, the reason wool always crackled on him in winter. Tide was new and belonged to a goddess. Storm was his.

Intellect 29 told him to keep it small. Half the town was standing around a hole in the ground, and nobody would thank him for setting the reeve's cloak on fire.

So he held up one hand, palm out, and pulled on the itch.

***Intellect check: 58% chance, rolled 3: triumph***

It came the way a sum comes out right on the first try. The itch ran down his arm, gathered in his palm and sat there: a knot of blue-white light the size of a walnut, crackling and perfectly still. It did not wander. It did not spit. It waited for him, like a dog that already knew its name.

On the Ledger, the blank line filled itself in. *Attuned: Storm.*

*Well. That settles that.*

The crowd went quiet. Then a carter at the back said, "Huh," in the tone people use for a good horse.

Ddraig let the spark go. It folded back into his hand and was gone. His mana had barely moved.

Somewhere at the back of his head, Ysmere laughed, enormous and not at all offended. "Lightning. Of course. Weather is weather, darling. Just don't stand still."

Dunmore had watched the whole thing with his arms folded. "Controlled," he said. "First try. That's rarer than you'd think." He looked at Ddraig the way he looked at a column of figures that came out better than expected. "The Tallyhouse pays more for Rifters who can do that. It also asks more of them."

On her beam across the rubble, the dusty Rifter had stood up. She was not smiling. She was taking notes in her head; Ddraig knew the look, because he had worn it all morning.

He could follow Dunmore while the Registrar was in a good mood. Or he could find out what the woman on the beam wanted before she decided for him.

### Turn 4

**Player:** Cross the rubble and ask the Rifter what she wants

The rubble was loose, and Ddraig took it slowly. The woman on the beam watched him come the whole way and did not get up to help.

Up close she was older than he had thought, somewhere past thirty, with a Rifter's forearms and a split lip that had already stopped bleeding. The dust on her was the same grey as the dust on him. That was not a coincidence.

"You were down there," Ddraig said.

"Third rope from the left. You fell past me." She said it like a fact on a form. "Senna Brask. I ran that crew."

Ran. Past tense. Ddraig did the sum and did not like it.

"I'm sorry," he said, and meant it.

"Don't be sorry. Be useful." Senna looked at his hand, the one that had held the spark. "The other voice, the one you turned down. He came to me after. Offered me what he offered you." She touched her chest, the way people do when something new is sitting there. "Last Rest. I can hear the dead now, if they're fresh. My crew is still talking."

*That's a sentence nobody should have to say on a Seedtide morning.*

"He asked me to pass something on," she went on. "He said he isn't angry with you." She paused. "I've known him one hour and I already know that's worse."

Ddraig's Resolve was 20. It was not built for this conversation. He kept his face still anyway, the way he did when a client brought him bad accounts.

"What do you want?" he asked.

"Four of mine are down there. The Oath says I see them buried properly. I can't haul four bodies up a broken Stair alone." She nodded toward the hole. "Your crew's sack is down there too. Dunmore wants it. I want my people. You light the way and help me carry, and I'll tell the Tallyhouse you found the sack. Your name on the find, not mine."

It was a good offer. It was also an offer to go back into a collapsing rift with someone who had just told him the dead were talking.

Behind him, Dunmore cleared his throat, the way a man does when he has been patient for exactly as long as he intends to be.

### Turn 5

**Player:** Agree to go down with Senna for her dead and the sack

Dunmore did not like it, but he liked an unregistered Elementalist dying in a hole even less. He gave them a rope, two lanterns from the Tallyhouse store and a time limit. "Before dark. After that I'm counting you as lost and charging the Ford for the rope."

Senna went first. She climbed like someone who had done this a thousand times and had stopped being impressed by it. Ddraig went second, with a lantern on his belt and a lot of opinions about ladders.

The Stair was not a stair any more. It was a slope of broken steps, mill timber and fresh stone, and every so often something below them settled with a long, unhappy groan. Vigor 30 was doing the work. His arms did not like it. They were going to tell him about it tomorrow.

Halfway down, the slope moved.

***Vigor check: 39% chance, rolled 16: success***

A slab under his left boot slid away into the dark. Ddraig had already shifted his weight onto the rope, the way Senna had told him to at the top, and he hung there, swinging, while the slab went down and down and finally stopped. He found the next foothold. He kept going.

*Senna was right. Rope first, feet second, pride third.*

At the bottom, the lantern light showed a pocket in the rubble, about the size of a small kitchen. The first of Senna's crew lay at the edge of it. Ddraig made himself look, because looking away felt worse. Senna knelt by the body and went very still, listening to something he could not hear.

The sack was three steps further in, wedged under a beam. It was canvas, tied with a Tallyhouse knot, about the size of a loaf. It hummed. Not loudly. Like a kettle just before it boils.

Ddraig, who hoarded small objects and knew it, wanted to pick it up very much.

"Don't," Senna said, without turning round. Her voice had gone strange. "Tam says don't open it. He says it's the reason the Stair came down."

Above them, the rift groaned again. Ddraig looked at the sack, at the dead man, at the rope going up into the dark, and started counting what they could carry in one trip.
